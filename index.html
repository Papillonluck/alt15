What is Travel Performance?

Travel Performance is health and wellness designed around the international traveller: how their journey, their state of mind and body on arrival and throughout their stay, and the purpose of their trip are considered together as one experience, not fragmented amenities.

It extends health and wellness across the wider guest experience, considering how it should work through the traveller’s journey, from before arrival through the stay and departure.

Helping guests feel well is what separates a hotel from its competitors.

The opportunity

The hotel is organised by department.The guest experiences one stay.

Three moments matter. Arrival. Stay. Departure. Most hotels sell only the middle one. Travel Performance turns all three into part of the reason to choose the hotel.

BEFORE & ON ARRIVAL
The journey and first hours
How the hotel can support the traveller before they arrive, anticipate how the journey may affect them, and shape the first hours after they reach the property.
THE STAY
The reason they came
The meetings, the stage, the deal. What they came to do happens during the stay, and the hotel either supports them around those moments or leaves it to chance.
DEPARTURE
How they leave
How the guest feels when they leave the hotel, what their onward journey demands of them, and how the experience can support them beyond the stay.

Guests don’t experience a spa, a gym and a restaurant. They experience one hotel, and how they feel throughout their stay.

The traveller

Every journey has a reason.The hotel decides whether they deliver it.

Business, an event or something personal. Whatever the reason for the trip, the hotel is part of a much bigger journey. How the guest feels throughout the stay shapes how well they can deliver on the trip’s purpose.

That starts with understanding why they are there, how the journey has affected their sleep, energy and recovery, and what they need from the stay. It ends with an experience designed around the traveller and the purpose of their trip.

The elements of Travel Performance

Nine areas shape how a guest performs.The hotel impacts more of them than it realises.

Travel changes sleep, energy, routine and recovery, while the reason for the journey stays fixed. These are the nine areas ALT‑15° considers across a property: what already exists, how it works together, and what may be missing.

01
SCHEDULE AND TIMING

The reason the guest travelled, when they need to perform, and how the hotel experience sits around it.

02
BEFORE ARRIVAL AND FIRST HOURS

What the hotel can understand about the traveller before they arrive, how it can prepare for their journey, and how those first hours shape the stay that follows.

03
LIGHT AND ENVIRONMENT

The room and the spaces around it, and how the environment changes across the guest’s stay.

04
SLEEP

How well the guest sleeps on the first night and the nights that follow, and what the room does to support it.

05
MOVEMENT AND FITNESS

How movement can support the guest, from restoring energy after the journey to preparing for the moments they need to perform.

06
RECOVERY

The spa, treatments and recovery capabilities already in the hotel, and where they could matter most across the stay.

07
NUTRITION

What the guest eats across the stay, from restaurants and room service to the minibar, and how that supports sleep, recovery and energy when they need it.

08
HYDRATION & REPLENISHMENT

How the hotel supports hydration and replaces what the journey depletes, from arrival through to departure.

09
DEPARTURE AND AFTER THE STAY

How the guest feels after leaving, what they remember about the stay, and whether the experience gives them a reason to return.

The commercial question

When wellness is presented as a collection of amenities,what makes yours different?

You have an international guest whose state has been changed by travel. Your hotel already invests across health and wellness, but is that investment being turned into a differentiated experience built around the traveller and their journey?

If every luxury hotel can buy cryotherapy, red light, recovery equipment, diagnostics, longevity programmes and beautiful spa facilities, owning the equipment becomes less distinctive.

The question becomes: who is it for, what problem does it solve, how does the whole hotel experience connect around the traveller, and why should they choose you?

ALT‑15

Longevity considers health over time.Travel Performance considers how the journey affects the traveller, and what they need from the hotel as a result.

Two guests can arrive at the same hotel at 7am in completely different physiological states.

A luxury Dubai hotel can have guests arriving on the same afternoon from London, New York, Mumbai, Singapore and Riyadh. Different journeys, different schedules, different states, into a property that may already have an extraordinary spa, gym, recovery, sleep, nutrition and room environment.

That’s the Travel Performance opportunity.

Does it give a particular guest a reason to book? Can it become an experience that carries a rate?

Can it drive demand into facilities already in the building? Does it give the commercial team something distinctive to sell against the comp set?

When it works, the guest uses the gym instead of walking past it and the hotel has an offer to sell rather than amenities to list. The guest understands why it matters to them and existing facilities become part of the reason to book the property, not simply something available once they arrive.

The risk is not that a spa, gym or recovery suite is under-explained. It is that another property turns similar capabilities into a clearer reason to choose them.

Built from both sides of the problem.

From institutional finance to luxury wellness.Travel Performance sits between the two.

I spent fourteen years in institutional finance, including investor relations at Peloton Partners, a London hedge fund start-up that grew to $2.6 billion AUM, which I was recruited into from Goldman Sachs by founder Ron Beller. Running global multi-city investor roadshows is where the problem became obvious: colleagues operating at the very top of their profession, losing days to travel and treating it as the cost of doing business.

I saw the problem from the traveller’s perspective before I ever approached it through wellness.

I left finance after helping launch four hedge fund start-ups and took a sabbatical in Australia. Bondi at 5am, full of people training before work, showed me how differently health could sit inside a working life. I came back to London in 2008, trained as a personal trainer and launched Liberté Fitness, teaching outdoor fitness in parks with a team of trainers and running beach wellness retreats, featured by Newby Hands in Harper’s Bazaar. I sold the business, and moved into luxury wellness travel, performance, supplements and travel health.

One career inside the world of the international business traveller, another working on the other side of the same problem.

ALT‑15° was built at the intersection of the two.

2011–2015
The problem was bigger than the hotel gym
I became one of London's early in-hotel personal trainers at The Cadogan on Sloane Street, now The Cadogan, A Belmond Hotel, with guests booked through the hotel concierge. By 2012 my clients included members of UAE royal families and time-poor executives.

By 2015 I realised training alone was not solving the problem. Travel was affecting sleep, nutrition, energy and performance before clients reached the hotel gym, if they used it at all. I began working across the wider journey for a Global Partner at a top-five executive search firm, considering the traveller’s schedule, sleep, nutrition, training and recovery as one experience. It was an early wellness concierge service.
2016–2017
From the hotel to the airport
In 2016 I developed and released a jet lag app on the Apple App Store, tested with athletes at Loughborough University.
In 2017 I extended that thinking beyond the hotel and into the wider travel journey. At the Travel Tech Lab, part of London & Partners, I won its pitch competition, run with Collinson Group, for a personalised airport health concierge built around the traveller’s journey. The judging panel included a director from Heathrow Airport.

2019–2021
Building for the international traveller
The airport concierge concept needed wearables, personalisation and AI that were not yet connected. So I built what could help the traveller now.
I developed JetFuel, a Travel Performance supplement range, with the late Dr Ian Perry, an Aviation Medicine Scientist and medical consultant of more than forty years who held the rare title of Academician of the International Academy of Aviation and Space Medicine, of which there are only two hundred in the world.
To take JetFuel to market, I raised $650,000, including one of the first R&D loans issued by Virgin StartUp.
I created the formulations with UK manufacturers and launched the range in luxury retail including Selfridges, MR PORTER and NET-A-PORTER, securing every listing directly, without distributors. There I met with Global Beauty Director Newby Hands to discuss developing the range, eight years after she had featured my earlier wellness retreats in Harper’s Bazaar.
JetFuel was also bought by five-star hotels and luxury travel and concierge companies for their guests, in hotel suites, VIP guest experiences, airport lounges and private jets. It was included in the GQ Men of the Year gift bag, and featured in Maybourne Group Magazine, the publication of Claridge’s, The Connaught and The Berkeley.
Selling into luxury hospitality meant being in it. I spent those years inside London’s five-star hotels, in conversation with general managers at Corinthia, Maybourne and Shangri-La, and appeared briefly in the BBC’s A Hotel for the Super Rich & Famous, filmed at Corinthia.
