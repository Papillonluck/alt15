A travel and circadian performance system. Identity direction.

The strapline tracks to lock flush with the wordmark, so the two form one block. The degree sits outside that measure, above the cap line. That lockup is the unit that goes on pack.

Type is Archivo, standing in for Suisse Int'l. The degree is drawn as a true circle rather than set as a glyph, so it holds its shape at 25mm on a foil stick.

The mark

The view. The gradient of the sky from 38,000 feet — near-black at the zenith, indigo through the middle, a pale band at the horizon. The gradient carries the world; each stick takes a flat solid from a different depth of it, so Rest is darkest and Health lightest. No gradients on foil.

Typographic hierarchy

Three registers doing three jobs. Headers carry authority — medium weight, caps, tracking opened right up. Timings and doses carry precision — regular weight, tabular numerals so every figure aligns down the column. Explanations carry context in an editorial serif, which is the only soft element and appears once.

This is what makes an in-room card read as a prescription rather than a brochure.

Travelling west lengthens the day, and the body adapts at roughly an hour and a half in that direction — faster than eastward, but still four days from a five-zone shift. The timings above are anchored to destination clock, not origin. Morning light and the first dose are the two strongest signals available; taken together they pull the rhythm forward more than either does alone.

What this is. A direction, not a finished identity. Set in a stand-in typeface and would be redrawn as custom letterforms before production.

What comes next. The mark becomes the constant in every environment frame — the same lockup, at the right scale, on the tray table, on the caddy, in the room.
