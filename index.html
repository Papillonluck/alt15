The cumulative effect

Across the year, not one trip at a time

ONE ROTATION · 30 SECONDS
ALT‑15
ONE DAY, COMPRESSED

Cross six time zones in six hours and local time changes immediately. Your body clock can take six days to catch up.

Crossing multiple time zones disrupts the body's circadian rhythm. The resulting jet lag can affect sleep, concentration, digestion and recovery. For frequent business travellers, the effects can be compounded by repeated journeys before the body has fully adjusted to the previous time zone.

Over sustained periods of international travel, the problem becomes cumulative. Instead of fully adjusting between journeys, the traveller can carry the effects of one trip into the working week, the next journey and eventually the next time zone. The effects tend to show up precisely where they are least useful: in decision-making, concentration and communication, during the meetings, negotiations and presentations the journey was made for.

And performance does not stop when the working day does. Returning home exhausted and needing days to recover takes time from the people the trip was ultimately supposed to benefit. The objective is not simply to perform during the journey, but to return with the energy to be present with your family and get the best from the life you have built around the business.

Built from both sides of the problem

Fourteen years in finance.Fifteen building the methodology.

Papillon Luck
FOUNDER, ALT‑15°

I spent fourteen years in institutional finance, including investor relations at Peloton Partners, the London hedge fund I was recruited into from Goldman Sachs by founder Ron Beller, which grew to $2.6 billion AUM. Running multi-city investor roadshows is where the problem became obvious: people operating at the very top of their profession, losing days to travel and treating it as the cost of doing business.

The problem was identified from inside the client group rather than from the wellness industry looking in.

I have worked in wellness since 2008, beginning in luxury wellness travel and retreats before moving into personal training, performance, supplements and travel health. One of the first Equinox members in London in 2012 and Barry's in 2013.

2011–2016
The origin
I left the world of start-up hedge funds for luxury wellness, and became one of London's early in-hotel personal trainers at The Cadogan, delivering through the hotel concierge. By 2012 my clients included members of UAE royal families and time-poor executives.

By 2015 I realised training alone was not solving the problem. Travel was disrupting sleep, nutrition, energy and performance before clients reached the gym. I began designing entire trips around performance for a senior executive at a global top-five executive search firm, coordinating itinerary, sleep, nutrition, training and recovery.

In 2016 that thinking became technology: a jet lag app released on the Apple App Store and tested with athletes at Loughborough University. It established the core logic, that adaptation could be scheduled rather than endured.
2017
The concept
While based at the Travel Tech Lab, part of London & Partners, developing the jet lag app, the idea expanded into airport wellness technology. I won the Travel Tech Lab pitch competition, run with Collinson Group, for a personalised airport health concierge using flight information, physiology and traveller wellbeing to build a health journey through the airport. The judging panel included a director from Heathrow.

2018
The methodology
The technology evolved into a complete travel performance methodology, developed with the late Dr Ian Perry. An Aviation Medicine Scientist and medical consultant of more than forty years, he held the rare title of Academician of the International Academy of Aviation and Space Medicine, of which there are only two hundred in the world.

It combined itinerary, circadian timing, nutrition, movement, recovery and personalised guidance, extending into which flights to take, the order of countries on complex trips and when to schedule the most important meetings. The work also reached elite US sport through a performance sports science coach for American Olympic athletes.
2019–2020
The products
The methodology was being delivered through personalised travel kits. I began developing my own formulations with UK manufacturers, connecting the methodology, the technology and the products into one system. The first range, powder formulations and botanical infusion teas, launched commercially across luxury retail and travel, including NET-A-PORTER and MR PORTER. Approximately $650,000 raised, including one of the first R&D loans issued by Virgin StartUp. Every listing secured directly, without distributors.

The online experience was built around the journey itself. A route calculator mapped multi-stop travel to time-zone load and returned exactly what a traveller needed for that specific trip. An immersive private-jet retail experience was proposed to Selfridges. Middle East expansion was in preparation when international travel closed.
2021–2023

Capsule formulations added to the powders and teas, developed, launched and stocked at Selfridges. Delivery formats reworked around how people actually travel, and the protocol reviewed against changing regulatory positions in the US and the Gulf.
