The commercial question

When wellness is presented as a collection of amenities,what makes yours different?

Planned or existing, is your wellness investment becoming a differentiated offer the right guests understand, value and choose?

If every luxury hotel can buy cryotherapy, red light, recovery technology, diagnostics, sleep programmes and beautiful spa facilities, owning the equipment becomes less distinctive.

What matters is the guest journey, from before arrival to after departure: who the offer is for, what need it meets and why guests should choose you. Without it, wellness becomes a menu of activities no one knows how to deliver.

That turns wellness into a commercial question.

  1. Does it give a particular guest a reason to book and return?
  2. Can it become an experience that carries a rate and lifts spend per guest?
  3. Can it improve utilisation of the wellness assets, including off‑peak hours?
  4. Does it give the commercial team a distinctive offer to sell to guests and corporate clients against the comp set?

Room rates alone can no longer carry growth. Since 2019, global revenue per available room has risen 19%, while booking costs per available room have risen 25%.* The hotels winning now are “bundling wellness, unlocking ancillary revenue”.**

How we work

Commercial Wellness Performance

Senior commercial expertise to help luxury hotels, resorts, private members’ clubs, hotel-club hybrids and hospitality developments make better wellness investment decisions, develop a compelling guest proposition and turn it into revenue.

Our Commercial Wellness Performance engagement runs for 90 days, taking your property through four stages: Audit, Define, Align and Activate. We work alongside senior leadership to develop a commercially compelling wellness proposition, while your team retains operational ownership. We act as an external senior sounding board as the proposition develops and decisions arise.

The 90 days is the engagement length, not a countdown to opening. Pre‑opening properties can start many months ahead, while positioning, pricing and packaging are still being planned, then continue with us month by month through to launch.

What sets ALT‑15° apart

ALT‑15° brings together wellness expertise and commercial strategy, two disciplines that rarely work together closely enough in hospitality.

My journey

I spent fourteen years in institutional finance, including investor relations at Peloton Partners, a London hedge fund start-up that grew to $2.6 billion AUM, which I was recruited into from Goldman Sachs by founder Ron Beller. Running global multi-city investor roadshows is where the problem became obvious: colleagues operating at the very top of their profession, losing days to travel and treating it as the cost of doing business.

I saw the problem from the traveller’s perspective before I ever approached it through wellness.

I left finance after helping launch four hedge fund start-ups and took a sabbatical in Australia. Bondi at 5am, full of people training before work, showed me how differently health could sit inside a working life. I came back to London in 2008, trained as a personal trainer and launched Liberté Fitness, teaching outdoor fitness in parks with a team of trainers and running beach wellness retreats, featured by Newby Hands in Harper’s Bazaar. I sold the business, and moved into luxury wellness travel, performance and supplements.

One career inside the world of the international traveller, another working on the other side of the same problem.

ALT‑15° was built at the intersection of the two.

2011–2015
The problem was bigger than the hotel gym
I became one of London's early in-hotel personal trainers at The Cadogan on Sloane Street, now The Cadogan, A Belmond Hotel, with guests booked through the hotel concierge. By 2012 my clients included members of UAE royal families and time-poor executives.

By 2015 I realised training alone was not solving the problem. Travel was affecting sleep, nutrition, energy and performance before clients reached the hotel gym, if they used it at all. I began working across the wider journey for a Global Partner at a top-five executive search firm, considering the traveller’s schedule, sleep, nutrition, training and recovery as one experience. It was an early wellness concierge service.
2016–2017
From the hotel to the airport
In 2016 I developed and released a jet lag app on the Apple App Store, tested with athletes at Loughborough University.
In 2017 I extended that thinking beyond the hotel and into the wider travel journey. At the Travel Tech Lab, part of London & Partners, I won its pitch competition, run with Collinson Group, for a personalised airport health concierge built around the traveller’s journey. The judging panel included a director from Heathrow Airport.

2019–2021
Building for the international traveller
The airport concierge concept needed wearables, personalisation and AI that were not yet connected. So I built what could help the traveller now.
I developed JetFuel, a Travel Performance supplement range, with the late Dr Ian Perry, an Aviation Medicine Scientist and medical consultant of more than forty years who held the rare title of Academician of the International Academy of Aviation and Space Medicine, of which there are only two hundred in the world.
To take JetFuel to market, I raised $650,000, including one of the first R&D loans issued by Virgin StartUp.
I created the formulations with UK manufacturers and launched the range in luxury retail including Selfridges, MR PORTER and NET-A-PORTER, securing every listing directly, without distributors. There I met with Global Beauty Director Newby Hands to discuss developing the range, eight years after she had featured my earlier wellness retreats in Harper’s Bazaar.
JetFuel was also bought by five-star hotels and luxury travel and concierge companies for their guests, in hotel suites, VIP guest experiences, airport lounges and private jets. It was included in the GQ Men of the Year gift bag, and featured in Maybourne Group Magazine, the publication of Claridge’s, The Connaught and The Berkeley.
Selling into luxury hospitality meant being in it. I spent those years inside London’s five-star hotels, in conversation with general managers at Corinthia, Maybourne and Shangri-La, and appeared briefly in the BBC’s A Hotel for the Super Rich & Famous, filmed at Corinthia.
2026
